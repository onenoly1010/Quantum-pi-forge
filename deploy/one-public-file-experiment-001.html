Can a public claim survive independent verification?

We're running QPF One Public File — Verification Experiment #001.

Give us one public URL or public repository path, and one precise claim about the artifact.

We independently inspect the public material at a recorded UTC timestamp and report what the evidence actually supports:

VERIFIEDthe evidence reproduces the claimed state.
NOT VERIFIEDit does not.
UNKNOWN / GATEDthe public evidence isn't sufficient to determine the claim.

The rules of the experiment

  • No account. No credentials. No upload.
  • No access to your private systems. Ever.
  • The result can be positive or negative. That's the point.
  • Silence from no one is measured too — we publish our own adoption baseline.

Precisely what we verify — and what we don't

"We verified the content of the named file at the stated timestamp. We did not verify authorship, accuracy of statements within the document, legal effect, or anything about systems the document describes."

Every result ships with fingerprints (qpfv0: result id, qpfpkg0: package id) that anyone can independently re-derive from the same public bytes, forever. If the document ever changes, the delta becomes provable against our pinned receipt.

How to enter

Reply where you found this invitation, or email onenoly11@proton.me with subject ONE PUBLIC FILE containing:

  1. one public URL or repository path;
  2. one precise claim about it (one sentence).

Preferred first artifacts: governance/policy documents, release notes, or small published release artifacts with checksums. Not accepted in experiment #001: anything requiring private access, chain-specific audits, or unscopeable claims.

Authority boundary
