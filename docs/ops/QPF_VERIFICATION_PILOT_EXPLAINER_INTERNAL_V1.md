# QPF Pilot #001 Explainer - Internal v1

**Status:** Internal comprehension draft. Not approved for publication or
external distribution.

## In one sentence

QPF helps a project owner turn one technical claim into a clear record of
what public evidence supports, what remains uncertain, and how anyone can
check the result.

## Why someone might care

Technical projects often make claims that are hard to check quickly:

- "Our software is deployed and matches this documentation."
- "This AI system has these limits."
- "This public component is locked or controlled in this way."

QPF does not ask someone to trust a summary alone. It produces a short
evidence trail that shows the claim, the checks, the result, and the limits.

## What the participant does

The participant provides:

1. One public technical claim they want examined.
2. The public links, source code, documentation, or deployment references
   needed to check it.

That is all. If the claim or references are unclear, QPF pauses rather than
guessing.

## What the participant receives

Within the internal pilot boundary, the participant receives one evidence
package containing:

- the exact claim examined;
- the public evidence and checks used;
- what was verified;
- what could not be verified or was unavailable;
- clear `verified`, `unverified`, `gated`, and `unknown` labels; and
- a concise explanation of the result's limits.

## What QPF does not promise

The pilot is not:

- a security audit;
- a legal opinion;
- investment advice;
- a guarantee that a project is safe or successful; or
- proof of future adoption, revenue, or outcomes.

## The question the pilot must answer

After receiving the package, the participant should be able to answer:

> Did this make one technical claim easier to understand, verify, or explain?

A "no" or "not enough" answer is useful evidence. It tells QPF what needs to
change before any broader offer is justified.
